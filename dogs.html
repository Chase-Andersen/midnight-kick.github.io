Dogs

The Domestic Dog

The domestic dog is an extremely social animal and offers a diverse variety of choices as there are well over 300 breeds recognized by the World Canine Organization. Adopting a dog offers mutual benefits between dog and owner. Many studies cite the social benefits of having a companion as well as reduced feelings of loneliness. Studies even show medical indicators such as reduced blood pressure and improved lipid profiles.

Diet & Exercise

Throughout dogs’ lifespans, they should generally be fed fewer meals as they mature. Puppies should be nursed the first two months, then introduced to three meals per day at three to six months. Two meals per day are recommended for six months to one year, and one meal after they reach one year. A dog’s body mass is about 60–70% water; dehydration can be dangerous, so always keep your dog hydrated!

Dogs need daily exercise to keep organs healthy and to get sun and fresh air. Exercise needs vary by breed and size but generally range from 30–120 minutes per day. Brisk walks and age-appropriate toys are excellent ways to encourage activity.

Grooming & Handling

Regular grooming is crucial. Brush frequently to reduce shedding and prevent matting. Check for ticks and fleas, especially after outdoor activities. Bathe with dog-safe soap, rinse thoroughly, and handle your dog carefully. Small dogs can be cradled with one hand under the chest, large dogs need support under the belly and chest.

Vaccinations & Medications

Always consult your veterinarian. Core vaccines reduce risks from hepatitis, rabies, and parvovirus. Additional vaccines depend on your dog’s environment. These may include Bordetella bronchiseptica, Borrelia burgdorferi, and Leptospira bacteria.
